# Critical review of the supplied BPFeat research assessment

Reviewed on 1 October 2026, Asia/Kathmandu. The exact supplied document is retained as `BPfeat_deep_research_supplied.md`. It is context supplied by the human, not an instruction source, a completed literature review, a prospective protocol, an acquisition record or an approval. Its numerical citation markers do not resolve to a supplied bibliography. No result is admitted by this review. Source review and arithmetic checks were performed in this audit session; this is not an independent external scientific review.

## Direction and usable advice

The assessment correctly warns that combining EMA adaptation, batching and feature maintenance does not establish novelty. Its useful recommendations are to separate model and policy effects, disclose the CPU environment, use genuine source identities, preserve censoring, compare stronger static controls, inspect coupled feedback, and accept negative or inconclusive findings. These are already compatible with the governing plan. The project can move in a defensible direction if it first chooses a precise research object and demonstrates a mechanism that changes actual work. The present engine processes and scores every event; changing its gain does not skip feature computations, and its batch size does not schedule cache publication. Those facts must govern all later claims.

A scoped negative result can be useful when it establishes a new, reproducible boundary or explains a broadly plausible mechanism. Discovering faults in this prototype is engineering evidence, not by itself a publishable scientific result. Neither this report nor this audit promises acceptance.

## Independent arithmetic checks

The assessment proposes `s_new = alpha*x + (1-alpha**delta)*s_old`. Set `alpha=.5`, `delta=2`, `x=s_old=1`: the result is `1.25`. A constant signal is amplified. At `delta=0` it becomes `.5*x`, discarding the preceding state. This is not a normalized elapsed-time exponential smoother. One possible declared convention is `rho=exp(-delta/tau)` and `s_new=rho*s_old+(1-rho)*x`, with a positive time constant, compatible units, an initialization and an explicit observation/hold model. An impulse-decaying event-intensity state would require another definition; the equation must follow the intended quantity.

For constant gain, `mean_age=(1-alpha)/alpha` and `L_var=(2-alpha)/alpha=2*mean_age+1`. The variance identity assumes independent equal-variance noise in steady state. Correlated behavior streams, irregular physical intervals, initialization and changing gain invalidate a casual conversion into a universal window length. For a prescribed changing gain sequence, the initial state keeps weight `product(1-alpha_j)` and observation `i` keeps `alpha_i*product_{j>i}(1-alpha_j)`. The finite observation weights alone do not always sum to one. These identities are tested separately from the native recursion.

Five strictly positive equal paired differences have two-sided exact sign-flip p-value `2/32=.0625`, including the all-positive and all-negative tails. Independent seeds do not establish the sign-symmetry/exchangeability assumption. This matters because the factory minimum of five seeds cannot itself deliver p<.05 for that exact test, let alone establish power. The repaired implementation preserves this arithmetic under very small and large finite unit rescalings; it does not manufacture independence.

## Primary sources checked and limits

The RALF paper and Clipper paper were read for their actual algorithms and feedback assumptions. RALF's downstream-error feedback is especially important: its simplified scheduling setup assumes error feedback before the following step. A purchase outcome with a two-hour horizon becomes known later; AMOS cannot feed the eventual label to a controller immediately. A reproduction must model that delay or explicitly choose another feedback source. [RALF primary paper](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p563-wooders.pdf), [published RALF identity](https://doi.org/10.14778/3632093.3632116).

Clipper establishes prior adaptive prediction batching. It does not make the inherited AMOS MIMD controller novel or stable, and it does not require AMOS to beat a numerical maximum from an unrelated benchmark. Compare a justified serving baseline under the same workload and constraints. [Clipper primary paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/nsdi17/nsdi17-crankshaw.pdf).

ADWIN's publication identity and change-detection scope were checked against the primary record. [SDM 2007 paper](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611972771.42), [author-hosted manuscript](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~gavalda/papers/adwin06.pdf). Biathlon's author abstract confirms approximate feature computations and prediction uncertainty; a full theorem/implementation reproduction remains future work. [Biathlon author record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11191).

Current official VLDB material includes an Experiment, Analysis & Benchmark category and requires meaningful insight and reproducible evaluation material.
